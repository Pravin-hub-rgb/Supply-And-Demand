**Part 2: Demand Supply**

- When we put a big order, there are some pending orders.
- Reason: every buyer needs a seller and vice versa.
- FII and DII orders are also pending.
- Sellers ↑ → price ↓
- Buyers ↑ → price ↑
- Sellers = Buyers → price no more moves ✱. No pending orders here. *(Orders get matched against each other, so nothing is left over. This is what a base looks like.)*

**Formula to identify base & exciting candles:**
- If body part > wick part → exciting candle
- Else → base candle

- If the body is more than 50% of the candle → exciting candle, **and also the price movement should be significant.**
  - The candle should not be small ✱
  - A candle bigger than the previous range = exciting.

## Page 4 (Part 3)

**Role of these exciting & base candles in our trading?**

- Institutions don't cancel their pending orders like us.

**Diagram 1 (demand zone):** a small base candle (buyers and sellers fighting) next to a tall green candle. A horizontal line marks the demand zone. Price falls back into the zone, then pushes back up when the pending orders get executed.
- Here the buying pending orders will be.
- But if the sellers are more, the zone will break.
- So we have to find these zones of pending orders.

**Diagram 2 (supply zone):** a base candle on top, marking the supply zone. Price drops, rallies back to the zone, and drops again. Later a "strong buy" breaks the base.

**Why zones work** ✱
- Institutions place orders too big to fill in one go, so part of the order stays pending at the base.
- The exciting candle that follows proves the imbalance was real.
- When price returns, the leftover orders execute and price bounces.
- Each return uses up some of the pending orders, so a fresh (untested) zone is strongest, and repeated tests weaken it until it breaks.

**Question:** What if the institution cancels the pending order, then what?
**Answer:** Important point → we cannot stop someone from buying and selling. That's why trading is a probability game. ✱ *(Zones sometimes fail, so use a stop loss behind the zone and trade only the best ones.)*

**Example:** SBI Monthly, Feb 14 ✱ *(probably February 2014)*
